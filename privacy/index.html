Your projects and your privacy

What goes where.

Input/output retention for service improvement is planned but is not enabled. The section below describes its intended default-on design, not collection happening today. Its safeguards, retention arrangements and legal basis must be finalised before activation. Cloud hub authorization and community submissions also remain subject to their launch checks.

Who to contact

Studworks is provided under the name studworks.build, based in Belgium. For privacy questions and requests, contact help@studworks.build.

The current website and support

The public website and Web App are hosted on DigitalOcean, which receives connection information such as IP addresses and request times to deliver the service. The service also uses connection information temporarily for security and request limits. See DigitalOcean's privacy information. We do not add advertising, cross-site tracking or individual visitor profiles. Network processing is separate from the limited improvement records described below.

If you email us, we receive your email address and the information you choose to send so we can respond. Do not send passwords, access tokens or private project details that are not needed to explain your question.

The Web App

Your current instructions, machine details and versions stay in this page’s memory. Download a reviewed backup before leaving if you want to keep them. The launch interface does not offer a saved-project library or file import. Older browser-saved copies are left untouched by this update; they are not uploaded, opened or deleted automatically. Clearing browser data can remove those older copies.

Making, checking or revising a draft sends the relevant project text to Studworks. Preparing a program also sends selected hub-check evidence and device roles. These requests are processed temporarily, not saved as a server-side project library. Planned improvement retention is limited to the scope below, not a cloud copy of your whole project.

No live Bluetooth connection or run approval is restored after a reload. An external connection does not grant permission for a physical run.

Improving Studworks — planned, not enabled

The planned service-improvement feature will be on by default when launched, without a separate opt-in or model-training permission checkbox. Its purpose is to understand common LEGO-building requests, find failures and confusing questions, and improve Studworks' model, grammar, clarification flow and corrections. This first-party analysis is not advertising, cross-site tracking or a profile of you.

The intended record contains a submitted instruction and the corresponding screened Studworks response: its answer, clarification questions, proposed behaviour, changes and generated program where available. We aim to preserve the admitted response rather than replace it with a summary. Records may also include a screened snapshot of the current build: mechanisms, directions, limits and behaviour before and after a correction; hub model and firmware; connected motor and sensor types, port positions and their described roles; and a small number of directly related exchanges. These are descriptive details, not connection credentials or permission to move hardware. Software/model versions and success, refusal or error information help explain the result. A program finishing is not the same as a builder confirming that the intended result happened.

We will not retain everything from a session or copy entire request/response objects. Improvement records are designed to exclude user or account IDs, visitor or session tracking IDs, IP addresses, HTTP headers, credentials, hub names, serial numbers and Bluetooth identifiers, project filenames, private notes, full project history, imported source files, raw console output and media. We will not collect keystrokes, clipboard contents, unsubmitted drafts or other applications' conversations. Related exchanges are bounded to the current building task, not linked through a persistent visitor identity. A random record reference may be used to manage an individual example and its deletion.

Instructions and generated output can themselves contain personal information. We will screen both directions and selected build context before saving, remove detected identifying or sensitive content, and mark omitted or changed content rather than silently treating it as a complete example. Unknown building terminology is not itself a reason to discard an instruction. Screening is not a guarantee that no personal information remains; records require restricted access and review before dataset use. Please do not include personal details, secrets or information about other people in your instructions.

Reviewed examples may be used for first-party evaluation and training to improve Studworks. Training data and independent test data will be kept separate. A generated answer, a completed program or the absence of a complaint will not automatically be treated as a correct example. The records will not be sold, published as a dataset or used to claim ownership of your projects.

The proposed retention limits are up to 30 days for records awaiting review and up to 90 days for reviewed source records. Access will be restricted to people operating and improving Studworks. Dataset, model-lineage and backup retention, deletion handling and the production location must be specified before activation; training will not begin without those arrangements.

We intend to assess legitimate interests for these defined improvement purposes, including whether retention is necessary and proportionate and how it affects builders, particularly children. This notice does not establish that assessment by itself. The feature will not be activated until that review and the safeguards are complete. Applicable rights to object or request access, correction or deletion remain; no account will be required. We plan a private per-record receipt to help locate retained exchanges, with its recovery limitations explained before launch. Deleting source records cannot be described as instant erasure from already-trained model weights.

Claude, ChatGPT and other tools

If you connect another AI service, it can receive the project and machine information covered by the access you allow. The online bridge temporarily relays proposals and results. Current AI-session permission lasts up to 30 minutes and can be ended locally. Each physical run still needs a separate local approval.

Your chosen provider has its own account, pricing, retention and privacy rules. Review those rules before choosing what to share with that provider.

Submitting a build

Sharing is optional. Submissions contain the title, description, hardware, instruction, photo or video link, permissions and any project file you choose to attach. You may choose anonymous credit or a name or username and an optional public blog or social link. That credit is content on the submission, not an account or a verified identity.

Submissions are stored privately for moderation. Only approved builds appear publicly. Attached project files can contain notes and earlier versions, so review them first. Do not include private contact details, location information or images of people without appropriate permission. Links take visitors to another website; Studworks does not control that website’s privacy practices.

Keep your private receipt: it lets you check the submission and withdraw it. Withdrawal removes the submitted content and attachment from the live gallery and database, leaving a minimal administrative record temporarily. It cannot remove copies already downloaded, search-engine caches or the original media on another site. With no builder email collected, a lost receipt cannot simply be emailed back.

Retention and service providers

The proposed online service uses DigitalOcean hosting and PostgreSQL for submissions and moderator access. Moderator sign-in emails use Resend. Hosting and mail providers necessarily handle connection or delivery information. The operator must confirm production regions, contractual safeguards, international transfers, access-log settings and backup retention before launch.

The implemented cleanup policy removes pending submission content after 90 days and rejected content after 30 days, then removes withdrawn records after a further 30 days. Moderation events expire after 365 days or when their associated withdrawn record is removed. Published builds remain until withdrawal. These deletions depend on a scheduled cleanup job; its operation and backup deletion schedule still require deployment verification.

Cookies, diagnostics and service improvement

Studworks does not add advertising or cross-site tracking. Essential cookies support browser authorization and moderator login. Moderator sessions expire after eight hours; sign-in links expire after five minutes. The Web App keeps its example-management preference and private receipts in browser storage. Older saved-project data may remain from previous versions, but the launch interface does not open or update it. The planned improvement feature will not introduce a visitor-tracking identifier.

The debug-report preview excludes instruction text, program output, project notes, names and credentials by default. Debug reports are not sent automatically. The separate raw console or request-copy tools can include private text; check it before sharing. Publishing a community build does not enroll its full project, attachments or media into improvement data. The planned input/output feature is a separate, narrowly defined processing path described above.

Your choices and requests

You can clear browser data, end an online connection and use your private receipt to access or delete a retained example or withdraw a submission. Contact the address above to ask about access, correction, deletion or other data-protection rights that apply to you. We may need enough information to locate the record and verify your authority without collecting unnecessary details.

Where applicable, your rights include access, correction, deletion, restriction, portability, objection and withdrawal of consent. You may also contact the Belgian Data Protection Authority or your local supervisory authority. A parent, guardian or teacher should handle public submissions for children. Production processing details and young-builder arrangements will be reviewed before online submissions open.
