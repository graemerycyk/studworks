03 / On the drawing board

A crossing that waits for the train.

A small piece of a railway with a life of its own.

A Studworks concept · not a completed build

Not available in this alpha. This is a design idea, not a runnable project or construction guide. Sensor-triggered automation and mechanism calibration still need implementation and physical qualification.

The idea

A train approaches, the barrier lowers, and the crossing waits until the train has passed before opening. The builder describes the sequence in words, rather than wiring together port-specific commands.

“When the train approaches, lower the barrier. Once it leaves, wait two seconds and raise it.”

  1. Approach
  2. Barrier down
  3. Departure
  4. Wait 2 s
  5. Barrier up

A possible workbench

  • A compatible hub, a motor for one barrier, and a supported sensor to detect the train.
  • A barrier with known permitted travel and room to move.
  • A train operated separately. This concept does not claim support for controlling an unencoded train motor or coordinating multiple hubs.

That is a candidate parts list, not a tested configuration. The sensor, its placement and the mechanism all need to be qualified together.

The interesting work is in the questions

  • Which motor is the barrier, and which direction lowers it?
  • How far may it travel without hitting a stop?
  • What counts as the whole train having passed—not just a gap between carriages?
  • What should happen when detection fails, a movement stalls, or the connection is lost?

The diagram describes an intention. Answering these questions is what turns it into a responsible physical behaviour.
