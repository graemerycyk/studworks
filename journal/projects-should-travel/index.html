Design note / Product direction

A project should travel.
Permission should not.

5 September 2026 · By Grae

Update — 7 September 2026. Portable projects, independent machine reuse and reviewed current-version sharing are now implemented in the native app and browser. The gallery supports downloadable projects and remix-source links once its backend is deployed. Use the Share your build form, not email; it stays closed until the service is available. Review Share current version before attaching it, and keep Download full backup for the retained history you want to save. Direct publishing from the app remains future work. The original design note below records where we started; the public beta and hosted features still await release.

A page of code can tell a motor on port A to turn. It cannot tell you whether your motor on A is attached to the same thing as the original builder’s.

That is the interesting problem behind sharing Studworks projects. We want to share what a machine should do, without silently carrying over assumptions about somebody else’s construction.

Share the intention

Studworks already has a shared representation of named roles and typed actions, separate from the port-specific Python that eventually runs on the hub. The native app and local MCP interface use that platform.

That gives us a starting point for portability. A future shared project could describe the barrier, then ask which motor controls it on your build. It would still need to check capabilities, direction, gearing and permitted travel. Matching a role to a port is only part of the job.

Keep permission local

A downloaded project must not arrive with permission to run. Neither a creator’s approval nor an AI agent’s confidence establishes that your mechanism is ready to move.

The intended workflow is to inspect, ask, adapt and review—then let the builder decide whether to run. Importing a file should not execute it, and unsupported behaviour must not be hidden inside an otherwise plausible project.

Make things before building a social network

The Projects pages started with manually written build notes. No accounts, feeds ranked by popularity, or invented community numbers. We initially invited builders to send their work by email so we could work out how to tell its story together; the update above links to the new submission flow.

At the time of this original note, publishing directly from the app, portable project downloads and “remix this build” were future work. A starter page let you copy an instruction into the normal Studworks workflow. It never connected to a hub or ran a program.

Keep the tool yours

The Studworks app remains free, forever, and works locally without an account. Sharing a creation should be optional, not the price of using your own hardware. External AI services connected through MCP have their own privacy terms; that does not change the app’s local-first design.

The goal is a useful shelf of things people have actually made. Start with a small experiment. Show what happened. Give the next builder enough information to make their own version.
