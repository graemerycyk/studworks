Field note / Testing

A battery reading, a red light, and a quarter-turn.

5 September 2026 · By Grae

The first Studworks test rig was deliberately small: a City Hub running Pybricks 4.0.1 and a Technic Large Motor 88013 on port A, with nothing attached to it.

That was enough to find problems that compiling cleanly could not reveal. At first, programs appeared to finish without useful output. Once the console showed the hub’s errors, we could fix assumptions that did not hold on its Python runtime. A port probe eventually reported the real hub and the motor on A.

A question should not become an interview

“Report the battery voltage” initially led to a question about what the motor was attached to. It was the wrong question. Reading a battery does not need to know whether a motor is a train or a barrier.

After the request-handling work, the same instruction produced a simple report. One run returned Battery: 8972 mV. A small result, but a useful one: the interpretation matched the request, and the answer came from the hub.

Finished is not the same as worked

“Turn the hub light red” generated a light command and reached “Program finished.” The light stayed blue.

That distinction matters. A successful software path is not the same as a correct physical result. After the light-control fix, the light visibly changed. We needed both the console and a pair of eyes.

One motor. One movement.

“Turn the motor 90 degrees slowly” prompted Studworks to confirm the motor. The generated action requested a 90-degree turn at 200 degrees per second, and the physical movement was confirmed.

It did not prove that Studworks could operate a crane, find a barrier’s end stops, or understand gearing. It proved that a particular unloaded motor could perform that bounded action through the development build.

What we can honestly claim

Those earlier sessions exercised discovery, battery reporting, a hub light, and bounded motor movement. They are useful development evidence, not a blanket qualification for every supported device or a newer exported app.

The current release still needs its own hardware checks, including stopping, disconnecting and quitting. Every new mechanism introduces facts the electronics cannot tell us.

For now, the first project notes start exactly where the evidence starts: meet the hub, then make one small turn. Bigger builds will earn their place here by working on the bench.
